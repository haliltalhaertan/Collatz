## Counting route

The stars-and-bars identity is valid under the uniform weak-composition law conditional on the endpoint. Each pair with total b has b+1 raw splits. Multiplying prefix, block, and suffix counts and dividing by the endpoint count gives the displayed identity. Zero-component conventions cover boundaries. Additional first-passage conditioning requires proof that the restarted law remains this same law; the model correctly flags this limitation.

The proposed constant conditional hazard is sufficient by iterated conditional expectation IF true, but is not a weaker consequence of the target. It is unsuitable as stated for all positive-probability prefix histories: a revealed low entry makes C_q impossible. Under the endpoint-only law such histories exist at interior starts (e.g. a zero prefix with remaining mass allocated to the suffix). Moreover a growing bounded-positive word has vanishing probability. To see this without independence, reveal its L pairs in any fixed order, using exchangeability. With n raw coordinates and s remaining mass, the conditional probability that the next pair is zero is (n-1)(n-2)/((s+n-1)(s+n-2)). For L=O(log R), n>=R-2L and s<=K=O(R), this is bounded below by a positive constant delta. Consequently P(G_q|S_R=K)<=(1-delta)^L, tending to zero. A fixed positive lower bound for C_q cannot hold under that law. This does NOT refute the global occupation estimate over many blocks.

## Cycles route

The even-parity algebra is correct: with u_j=H_j-jK/J and d=K-2 beta J, log_2 z_j=a+u_j+jd/J. Pair weights are proportional to product(b_j+1), hence rotation invariant under endpoint conditioning. This does not establish the affine-barrier occupation law or preservation of extra conditioning. Conditioning an odd singleton on value s changes the pair endpoint to K-s; uniform estimates cannot silently discard that summation.

Dropping marks and counting all non-low pair entries gives a weaker necessary Laplace target (up to endpoint-index constants). No proof of that target was produced. This is mainly an accurate restatement of the known obstacle.

## Low-state route

The segment counting formula and fixed zero-prefix entrance identity are correct under the endpoint-only bridge law. A claim of a Gaussian limit by a direct Stirling expansion is not a displayed uniform proof here and is not newly certified by this review.

The proposed probability bound P(M<a log R)<=C/R is not automatically a weaker consequence of a Laplace bound at the same scale. From E exp(-lambda M)<=C/R, Markov's inequality gives only P(M<a log R)<=C R^(lambda a-1). Dropping marks weakens the counted object, but strengthening its requested lower-tail rate can offset that. Thus the model's 'strictly narrower' wording does not establish logical implication. The proposed inequality remains an additional unproved route condition.

## Resonance route

The one-block coefficient count reproduces the existing exact reduction; counting independently chosen prefix/block/suffix configurations does not assert stochastic independence after conditioning.

Let N_G count bounded-positive, non-low blocks; let N_res count the resonant subset, so N_C=N_G-N_res exactly. A valid sufficient decomposition is

E exp(-lambda N_C) <= P(N_G<A log R) + P(N_res>N_G/2, N_G>=A log R) + R^(-lambda A/2).

Thus O(1/R) bounds for BOTH probabilities, with A>=2/lambda, would suffice. Luna's A>=4/lambda is conservative. Neither probability bound was proved.

Its claim that the resonance masking bound alone is 'strictly weaker than the target' is unjustified. As a logical counterexample on abstract counts, choose deterministic N_C=ceil(log R/lambda), N_G=ceil(B log R), N_res=N_G-N_C, with B>max(A,2/lambda). Then the Laplace target holds, while the masking event has probability one for large R. These abstract counts test logical implication only; they are not an actual accessible Collatz counterexample. The decomposition is a useful way to organize obligations, not a new theorem.

## Refutation route

The final output supplies no actual accessible counterexample, and correctly observes that a fixed low prefix does not suppress a global marked occupation count. Its proposed P(N_C<A log R)<=C/R with A>1/lambda is sufficient by splitting the Laplace expectation at A log R. However it is a stronger sufficient condition, not a weaker necessary lemma or demonstrated simplification. For example deterministic N_C=ceil(log R/lambda) already satisfies the target but fails that tail bound for every A>1/lambda and large R. This abstract example concerns logical implication only. The conditioning-law ambiguity it flags also appears in the counting route.
