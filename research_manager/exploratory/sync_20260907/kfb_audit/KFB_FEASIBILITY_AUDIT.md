# Independent audit: fixed-start killed Feynman--Kac feasibility

Date: 2026-09-07  
Audited object: `../kfb/KFB_FEASIBILITY.md`  
Scope: static mathematical audit only. No sealed or historical numerical run,
Git/Drive operation, canonical edit, or depth extension was performed.

## Verdict

**PASS WITH MAJOR SCOPE/WORDING REPAIRS.**

The endpoint algebra, free negative-binomial mass, and exact domination

```text
|K_m^h(a,b)| <= Q_m^h(a,b)
```

are correct for the stated admissible fixed-start arrays. The free denominator
has the required uniform `m^(-1/2)` scale by a direct local Stirling estimate.

The killed numerator bound in `(BLL)`, however, is neither cited nor proved in
the audited note. Its applicability is plausible under a sufficiently general
one-dimensional killed local-limit theorem, but it is not automatic from the
phrases "span one" and "standard ballot/local limit": the centred increments
live at time `m` on the shifted coset `-beta*m+Z`, and the barrier becomes an
irrational, time-dependent integer boundary after reduction to the integer sum.
The invoked theorem must explicitly cover this arithmetic-shift formulation and
give constants uniform over the actual admissible compact endpoint array.
Consequently `(KUB)` is **conditional on `(BLL)` as the document currently
stands**, not an unconditional proved corollary of material supplied there.

After `(BLL)` has been supplied, `(XUB)` is a sufficient single additional
estimate for the chosen fixed-start split. Before that, there are two open
obligations: the positive killed estimate `(BLL)` and the crossing estimate
`(XUB)`. Moreover `(XUB)` is sufficient rather than logically necessary, since
a direct estimate of the sum of the killed and crossing pieces could exploit
cancellation between them.

The claimed impossibility result must be narrowed to **finite stationary state
quotients on which the pointwise multiplier descends**. That restricted claim
is correct. The text does not rule out every conceivable exact finite
reduction, for example a time-inhomogeneous recurrence or an aggregate
coefficient identity that does not represent `q(x)` pointwise.

The short-range inequality `(SR)` is correct. The subsequent Fredholm language
is only a heuristic: no function space, compactness statement, Fredholm
operator, or nonresonance condition is established. It must not be presented
as a reduction already obtained.

No route-changing theorem or countertheorem was found. Nothing here proves
`G=O(1/r)`, E6-N2, B4, a nonzero profile, a polynomial lower bound, or Collatz.

## 1. Endpoint and free-mass audit

Put

```text
alpha=log_2(3), beta=alpha-1, rho=beta/alpha.
```

Then `1-rho=1/alpha` and

```text
rho/(1-rho)=beta,
```

so `Y-beta` is centred when
`P(Y=y)=(1-rho)rho^y`, `y>=0`.

For the `G` family,

```text
m=r,
a=-4-alpha,
b=-12-alpha-theta_r,
b-a+beta*m=-8-theta_r+beta*r=floor(beta*r)-8=n_r.
```

For `H_0`,

```text
m=r-4,
a=-5*alpha,
b=-13-beta-theta_r,
b-a+beta*m
 = -13-beta-theta_r+5*alpha+beta(r-4)
 = beta*r-theta_r-8=n_r.
```

Thus both arrays use the same nonnegative integer total exactly when
`n_r>=0`. The reported threshold `r>=14`, with `n_r=0` at `r=14,15`, is
correct.

If `k=b-a+beta*m` is a nonnegative integer, independence gives

```text
p_m(a,b)=binom(k+m-1,m-1)(1-rho)^m rho^k.
```

Here `k=beta*m+d_m`, where `d_m` stays in a fixed bounded interval for each of
the two arrays. Uniform Stirling expansion around the mean of the negative
binomial law therefore gives

```text
p_m(a,b_r)
 = (2*pi*alpha*beta*m)^(-1/2) (1+O(1/m)),
```

uniformly over the actual array (the bounded offset only changes the expansion
at relative order `O(1/m)`). In particular the lower half of `(BLL)`,
`p_m(a,b_r)>=c_1*m^(-1/2)`, is independently justified for all sufficiently
large admissible `m`; finitely many earlier admissible cases can be absorbed
into `c_1`.

The phrase "uniform in theta" must not mean arbitrary `theta in [0,1)`.
For fixed `a,m`, only endpoints satisfying
`b-a+beta*m in Z_{>=0}` carry mass. The uniform family is the actual
triangular array generated by `theta_r={beta*r}`.

## 2. Exact operator convention and domination

The displayed operator

```text
(P_h f)(x)=1_{x<=h} sum_y p(y)f(x+y-beta)
```

tests the departure state. Hence its `m`-th power tests
`X_0,...,X_(m-1)<=h`, whereas the definition `tau_h>m` also tests `X_m<=h`.
For the actual arrays and `h=0`, `b_r<0`, so the two events coincide and the
kernel identities are correct. For the document's broader phrase "every fixed
barrier", one must either assume `b<=h` or put an arrival indicator into the
kernel; otherwise the stated identity with `tau_h>m` is not literally valid.

On an admissible endpoint with positive free mass,

```text
L_m^h(a,b)
 = E_a[prod_(t=0)^(m-1) q(X_t); X_m=b, tau_h>m].
```

Since every factor has modulus one,

```text
|L_m^h(a,b)|
 <= E_a[1; X_m=b, tau_h>m]
 = p_m^h(a,b).
```

Dividing by `p_m(a,b)>0` proves exactly

```text
|K_m^h(a,b)| <= Q_m^h(a,b).
```

This proof is unconditional and does not require renewal theory. For
inadmissible endpoints the conditional quotients are undefined, not zero;
only the unnormalised kernels can consistently be declared zero there. The
note should avoid defining `Q` or `K` by division when `p_m=0`.

The marker construction also needs a normalization sentence: the coefficient
`[z^k]T_h(z)^m` includes the factor `(1-rho)^m rho^k` and equals the
unnormalised killed mass for the endpoint tied to `k`. With that convention it
is correct.

## 3. What `(BLL)` still requires

The required positive statement is

```text
p_m^0(a,b_r) <= C_1*m^(-3/2)
```

for either fixed start and the actual accessible endpoints in a fixed compact
interval below zero. Its expected scale is consistent with the usual
one-dimensional killed local limit: a free central mass of order
`m^(-1/2)` multiplied by a bridge survival probability of order `m^(-1)`.

There is nevertheless a real theorem-matching obligation. Although the
increment law is supported on the arithmetic coset `-beta+Z`, it is not a
zero-shift integer-valued centred walk. At time `t`,

```text
X_t in a-beta*t+Z.
```

Equivalently, in terms of the integer partial sum `S_t`, killing at zero is the
moving-boundary condition

```text
S_t <= beta*t-a.
```

Because `beta` is irrational, the integer boundary is
`S_t<=floor(beta*t-a)` and changes aperiodically with `t`. A theorem stated
only for an integer-valued centred walk killed at a fixed integer boundary
cannot be invoked without an additional transformation or proof. A theorem
for general real-valued random walks with arithmetic local probabilities may
well apply directly, but the audit needs its exact hypotheses and a uniformity
statement for accessible `b_r` in the compact terminal interval.

Required closure options are therefore either:

1. cite a killed local-limit theorem for real-valued/arithmetic-shift random
   walks and verify its centring, variance, moment, accessibility, strict versus
   weak killing, and compact-uniform endpoint hypotheses; or
2. give a direct ballot/ladder proof for the boundary
   `floor(beta*t-a)` with constants uniform for the two fixed starts and actual
   terminal offsets.

Until one of these is done, the correct classifications are:

- free lower local limit: **`PROVED IN THIS AUDIT`**;
- killed upper local limit: **`STANDARD-LOOKING BUT UNSUPPLIED / OPEN AS A
  DOCUMENT OBLIGATION`**;
- `(KUB)`: **`PROVED CONDITIONAL ON (BLL)`**.

If `(BLL)` is supplied, domination immediately yields

```text
|K_m^0(a,b_r)|
 <= p_m^0(a,b_r)/p_m(a,b_r)
 <= (C_1/c_1)/m.
```

No complex Feynman--Kac coefficient asymptotic is needed for this upper bound.

## 4. Finite-state claim

There is a clean restricted impossibility statement. Along even the all-zero
forward path from either fixed start,

```text
x_t=a-beta*t,
2^(x_t)=2^a(2/3)^t.
```

For all sufficiently large `t`, these numbers are distinct elements of
`(0,1)`, so `q(x_t)=exp(2*pi*i*2^(x_t))` are also distinct and converge to
one. Thus no finite stationary quotient of the reachable states can preserve
the pointwise multiplier through a rule `q(x)=qbar([x])`: such a quotient
would allow only finitely many multiplier values.

This proves less than the note's unqualified sentence "no exact finite-state
reduction". It excludes the natural exact Markov/state quotient preserving
every row multiplier. It does not exclude, without further definition and
argument:

- a finite-dimensional but explicitly time-dependent recurrence;
- a finite recurrence for one aggregated coefficient family;
- an algebraic identity that sums phases without making `q` descend
  pointwise; or
- a finite automaton whose state carries a time/depth parameter outside the
  finite state label.

The density/nonperiodicity argument in the note can support the restricted
quotient claim, but it cannot quantify over undefined notions of "reduction".
The verdict should say **no exact finite stationary pointwise-multiplier state
quotient**, not no exact finite reduction of any kind.

## 5. Short range, resolvent, and Fredholm language

For `x in (h-ell-1,h-ell]`,

```text
|q(x)-1| <= 2*pi*2^x <= 2*pi*2^(h-ell),
```

and hence

```text
sum_(ell>=0) sup |q(x)-1|
 <= 2*pi*2^h sum_(ell>=0)2^(-ell)
 = 4*pi*2^h.
```

Thus `(SR)` is correct as a depth-shell summability estimate.

It does not by itself imply that `T_h-P_h` is compact, trace class, or a
Fredholm perturbation on any relevant space. Each bounded depth shell contains
infinitely many reachable states in the union over times, no Banach/Hilbert
space or reference measure is specified, and the transition has unbounded
upward jumps. A genuine Fredholm route must define the space and show the
required compactness/relative compactness and zero-energy invertibility.

There is also a structural warning about the displayed fixed-endpoint Green
kernels. If both `m_1` and `m_2` connect the same exact `a,b`, then

```text
(k_1-k_2)=beta*(m_1-m_2).
```

Irrationality of `beta` forces `m_1=m_2`. Therefore, for fixed exact real
`a,b`, the series `sum_m s^m P_h^m(a,b)` has at most one nonzero term. The
operator resolvent may still be meaningful globally, but this displayed
fixed-point kernel is not, by itself, the usual multi-time renewal series that
the prose suggests. A useful renewal/Fredholm formulation must instead choose
an appropriate state/time grading, boundary coordinate, or reference kernel.

Accordingly:

- `(SR)`: **`PROVED`**;
- "boundary-layer renewal/Fredholm treatment is plausible": **`HEURISTIC`**;
- an actual Fredholm or scalar Wiener--Hopf reduction for `T_h`: **`NOT
  ESTABLISHED`**.

## 6. Is `(XUB)` the one remaining estimate?

For `b_r<0`, the events `tau_0>m` and `tau_0<=m` partition the conditioned
bridge paths, so

```text
Psi_m(a,b_r)=K_m^0(a,b_r)+R_m^0(a,b_r)
```

exactly, with `R` equal to the conditional expectation in `(XUB)`. Hence:

- `(BLL)` plus domination gives `K=O(1/m)`;
- `(XUB)` gives `R=O(1/m)`;
- the triangle inequality then gives `Psi=O(1/m)`.

Thus `(XUB)` is indeed the single remaining estimate **after** `(BLL)` has
been separately closed, and it is a clean next arithmetic target for this
particular two-piece strategy. As the document is currently written, calling
it the sole missing estimate suppresses the still-unsupplied `(BLL)` theorem.

The crossing probability tending to one explains why a positive ballot bound
alone cannot establish `(XUB)`: the estimate must exploit phase cancellation
or some direct cancellation between the two pieces. But the stronger wording
that `(XUB)` is "exactly" necessary should be avoided. One may instead prove
`Psi=O(1/m)` directly or obtain a joint asymptotic in which `K` and `R`
cancel. `(XUB)` is sufficient and strategically isolated, not logically
unique.
